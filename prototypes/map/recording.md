# Reading sheet — provocation half

Fill this in **before watching either video**. A sanitised log tests nothing; that is the one
rule Step 2's diary method actually ran on.

## Kill condition

> If reading the map of an unwatched source produces no question — if the honest reaction is
> "just tell me what's in it" — branch 5 is wrong, and the map is not the on-ramp to the
> product. Say so and stop; **do not rebuild the map until it feels better.**

Weaker but still fatal: a question forms both times, but it is the *same* question ("what's
this about"), which is a summary request wearing a question mark.

## Rules

1. **Fresh sources only.** Not the three from Step 2, not the six from Step 1. A question
   cannot form honestly about a video whose contents are already known.
2. **Do not open the transcript.** The map is the whole stimulus. Opening the transcript is
   the thing the thesis says nobody should have to do.
3. **Write the reaction before watching.** Then watch if you want; the second row is optional
   and is not evidence, it is only a check on whether the question was worth asking.
4. One source read `--strict`, one read in default. Different sources on purpose — reading
   both renders of the *same* source ruins the second reading.

## Both sources have no skippable regions

Checked before reading, by cue count over both transcripts: zero sponsor phrases, zero outro
phrases in either; one passing "subscribe" in B, which correctly did not fire. Neither source
carries an ad read or a sign-off.

So the skip layer — Step 1's cheapest and most reliable result — contributes **nothing** here,
and the `--strict` render is reduced to bare time ranges. The sources were not swapped to
avoid this: re-picking until the render flatters itself is the move the kill condition
forbids. Weigh it when reading B.

### The strict map cannot have supplied the subject

The word "AI" does not appear anywhere in this map. Nothing does: twenty time ranges, no
skippable marks, no verbatim lines, no terms. Everything capable of producing the question —
the marketing subject, the speaker's name, his relevance to AI — came from the **title**,
which is metadata available from YouTube without Yoinks existing.

So there is a plain reading in which the map did no work at all here, and the control that
would rule it out was never run: **nobody checked what the title alone provokes.** Asked
directly, the reader could not say, having already read the map — the control is no longer
runnable on this source.

The same doubt reaches back to Source A. How much of the geopolitics question came from
*"Is Israel repeating the Crusaders' fatal mistake?"* rather than from the map beneath it?

### Both questions came out in the same shape

- A: *"How does this relate to the current geopolitics and what Professor Jiang is saying?"*
- B: *"How does this relate to Rory Sutherland's view of current AI space?"*

The same six opening words, and the same form: *how does this relate to ⟨thing I already
track⟩*. Put to the reader as a candidate for the weaker fatal case, this was judged
**genuinely different** — each named a specific, different referent rather than asking for an
account of the source. The observation is recorded next to the judgement so a later reader can
disagree with it.

## Verdict

n=2. Both maps read cold, neither video opened first.

**The kill condition did not fire.** Both maps produced a question, and neither question was
"just tell me what's in it". On the weaker fatal case — always the same question — the shared
template was put to the reader and judged genuinely different. On its own terms, branch 5
survives.

It survives badly, for three reasons the kill condition did not think to name.

### 1. Neither map got the reader to point at a part — 2 of 2

Branch 5's claim is not merely that a map provokes *a* question. It is:

> Someone looking at where a source changes subject knows which part they want, and asks for
> it — which turns the questionless majority into the case above.

Both readings recorded the provoking segment as **"overall"**. Neither question selects a
segment; both ask how the whole source relates to something outside it. Answering either one
needs the entire transcript back, which is the opposite of the narrowing the map exists to do.
The questionless arrival was not converted into the question-shaped case. It became a
different, broader thing that the thesis has no path for.

This is the sharpest result of the run, and it is consistent across both sources and both
renders.

### 2. The render made no observable difference

The rich render carries verbatim turn lines and distinctive terms. The strict render carries
twenty time ranges. Both produced a question, of the same form, at the same whole-source
granularity, with the same "overall" attribution.

Two readings of that, and the evidence does not separate them:

- The handles are unnecessary — "structure, never content" is enough, and the verbatim lines
  and terms added in `README.md` are unjustified by this test. They were my addition and this
  run does not earn them.
- **Neither render did anything, and the title did the work both times.** The strict map
  contains no subject matter at all, so it cannot have supplied "AI"; the reader, asked
  directly, could not rule out that the title alone would have produced the same question.

The second reading is the more damaging and cannot be dismissed. It also applies to Source A.

### 3. The skip layer contributed nothing

Neither source contains a sponsor read or an outro — verified by cue count before either map
was read. Step 1 called skip patterns "the cheapest and most reliable result of the whole
validation exercise", on one creator's uploads. Across two long-form interview sources it is
absent. If the sources people bring are podcast-shaped, half of what the thesis calls a map is
not there to be built.

### The test that should have been run and was not

**Title alone versus title plus map, on a fresh source.** Record the question the title
provokes; then show the map; record whether the question changes. Without it, this run cannot
distinguish "the map provoked a question" from "the person read a title and brought their own".
It is cheap, it needs no code that does not already exist, and it is now the load-bearing
unknown — ahead of anything about how the map is rendered.

It has to run on a source neither map has been shown for. Both of these are burned.

### Caveats

n=2, one person: the maintainer, who wrote the thesis being tested and knew what was being
measured. Source A's reaction was first filed against the wrong source, and its "summary
request in disguise" answer was reversed on review — both corrections moved in the favourable
direction and are recorded above rather than applied quietly. Source A's question was only
**partly** provoked by its map, by the reader's own account. The gate in
`docs/product-thesis.md` — three non-maintainers, one week — is not satisfied by this and was
never going to be.

**What this does not license.** The picker's second group, the two-list refactor, the
assistant-on-PATH shell-out. All of it stays behind the Measure. Two whole-source questions
from the author of the thesis, with the title-alone control unrun, is not the evidence that
unfreezes the document.
